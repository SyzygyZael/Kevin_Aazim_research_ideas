######################################################################
##  READ THIS FIRST  --  THE TWO INSTRUCTIONS THAT MATTER MOST      ##
######################################################################

(1) Bro matters most. THE FLOOR IS NEVER COMPUTED FROM A MODEL. The sufficiency floor
    Phi = E[Var(P|R)] is computed from the DATA (SPICE labels) grouped
    by representation-equivalence key -- NO training involved. If you
    ever find yourself "estimating the floor" from a trained network's
    residuals, STOP: that measures the gap, not the floor, and destroys
    the paper's central claim. Floors come only from
    simboundary_analysis.partition_floor / neighborhood_floor on the
    released SPICE labels. Trained models are used ONLY to measure how
    close achievable error gets to that data-derived floor.

(2) RUN THE POSITIVE CONTROL (E1) BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE, AND BELIEVE IT.
    E1 predicts DC gain, which the framework says has a LOW floor and is
    nearly solved by a plain 1-WL GIN. If E1 does NOT come out easy
    (low floor at R2, best-GNN RMSE within the floor's CI), your
    pipeline is broken (wrong labels, wrong keying, leaked test set,
    mis-scaled targets). Do not run or trust E2-E5 until E1 passes. A
    "high floor" result is only credible once the instrument has been
    shown to return "low" where low is expected.

Everything else below is standard. These two are the ones that, if
skipped, silently invalidate the whole study.

    * D-VAE, DAGNN  (DAG encoders, OCB)            Zhang 2019; Thost 2021
  TIER 2  SOTA circuit predictors (the real ceiling):

  from their released code and use DICE's released pretrained weights

  the training-free floors are the headline and need no GPU.

1. LADDER CONSTRUCTION  (do this once, verify by counting)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Build node/edge features for each rung R0..R4 (paper Table 2) directly
from the released netlists:
  R0 bare topology     : connectivity only
  R1 typed topology    : + component-type label
  R2 sized graph       : + OCB-native quantized device values
  R3 ported netlist    : + terminal role (gate/drain/source, +/-)
  R4 ported + flow     : + edge orientation (as in Flow-DAGNN)
On FALCON, the native net-level graph gives R1 and the terminal-
annotated netlist gives R3 (this is exactly the net-level vs terminal-
level contrast Asadi et al. 2025 study). On OCB, R2 uses CktGNN's native
quantization and R3 adds terminal roles.
For each rung, compute equivalence keys with
  simboundary_analysis.graph_key(adj, node_feats)
and REPORT the number of classes and the coverage (fraction of circuits
in non-singleton classes) BEFORE computing any floor. Sparse coverage at
R3/R4 is expected and must be disclosed, not hidden.

2. EXPERIMENTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

--- PILOT (cheap, do first, ~minutes on CPU) ---------------------------
P0. On Ckt-Bench-101 (the cheap benchmark), compute partition_floor at
    R0, R1, R2 for GAIN only. No training. Confirm floors are finite,
    decrease R0>=R1>=R2, and that gain's R2 floor is small relative to
    total variance. Purpose: sanity-check keying + labels before any GPU
    time or any FALCON download.
    Flag-back: if floors are non-monotone or coverage is ~0 at R2,
    the keying or quantization is wrong -- fix before proceeding.

--- E1  POSITIVE CONTROL (run first; gate for everything) --------------
  Objective : show the instrument returns "easy" where easy is expected.
  Data      : the most reliably-easy metric on each benchmark --
              DC POWER on FALCON (reported well under 1% rel. error) and
              DC GAIN on Ckt-Bench-101 (op-amp gain is well predicted).
              Do NOT use FALCON gain-type metrics as the control: the RF
              SOTA reports them among the HARDER metrics (that is part of
              what E2 investigates, not a control).
  Method    : partition_floor at R2 (data only); train GIN, DAGNN,
              Flow-DAGNN at R2 (10 seeds).
  Metrics   : floor Phi + bootstrap CI + coverage; best test RMSE and
              Pearson R over the encoders; gap = err - floor.
  Expected  : Phi(R2, .) near zero (small fraction of total var);
              best GNN RMSE within/near the floor CI; gap small.
  Supports  : validity of the estimator + pipeline.
  GATE      : if this fails, STOP and debug. Do not report E2-E8.

--- E2  FLOOR CURVES (main result) -------------------------------------
  Objective : locate, per property, where the floor reaches near zero.
  Data      : FALCON (primary), then OCB Ckt-Bench-101/301 (replication).
  Method    : partition_floor at R0..R4 for
                FALCON: P in {gain, bandwidth, noise figure, DC power}
                OCB   : P in {gain, bandwidth, phase margin, FoM}
              neighborhood_floor as cross-check at R3/R4 where coverage
              is low (report kappa-sensitivity).
  Metrics   : floor curve ell->Phi(R_ell,P) with CIs + coverage;
              permutation null per rung (ratio to total var ~1).
  Expected  : monotone-decreasing curves; gain and DC power below
              tolerance by R2-R3; NOISE FIGURE clearly ABOVE tolerance
              at R4 (device noise models + matching not in the graph).
              Noise figure is the leading high-floor candidate and it is
              measured from NATIVE FALCON labels (no re-simulation).
  Produces  : Figure 3, Table 4 (floor columns).
  Supports  : the graph-determined vs. simulation-required split.

--- E3  GAP vs FLOOR ---------------------------------------------------
  Objective : show expressiveness closes gaps, not floors.
  Data/Method: at each rung, best GNN error across the FULL encoder
              ladder (Tiers 1-3, including GraphGPS); gap = best error
              - floor, with a CI (propagate floor CI + model-error var).
  Expected  : which term dominates is unknown a priori (that is the whole
              point). We expect >=1 property gap-dominated at coarse rungs
              (error falls as expressiveness rises, toward the floor) and
              >=1 floor-dominated (error plateaus above the floor for
              EVERY encoder, including the GraphGPS transformer).
  Produces  : Table 3 (best-GNN + gap columns).
  Supports  : Theorem 2 + Corollary 2 (expressiveness closes the gap,
              never the floor); Corollary 1 (even a transformer >= floor).

--- E4  COLLISION CASE STUDIES -----------------------------------------
  Objective : make the floor mechanistic, not statistical.
  Method    : at R3/R4, extract R-equivalent circuit pairs with large
              property spread; identify the responsible physics
              (bias-dependent g_m, device noise, parasitics, mismatch).
  Expected  : a small gallery of "same graph, different SPICE" pairs.
  Supports  : interpretability; pre-empts "artifact of your partition".

--- E5  CROSS-BENCHMARK CONSISTENCY ------------------------------------
  Objective : show the boundary is a property of representation+physics,
              not of one dataset.
  Method    : for the SHARED properties (gain, bandwidth), compare the
              minimum sufficient rung and floor-curve shape on FALCON
              vs OCB.
  Expected  : the qualitative per-property pattern agrees across two
              families and two graph conventions. Disagreement = scope.
  Supports  : generality of the finding.
  Note      : Re-simulation via OCB's simulator is needed ONLY for a
              property NEITHER benchmark labels (e.g., PSRR); FALCON
              labels noise figure and DC power natively.

--- E6  DECOMPOSITION vs THE LEADERBOARD (the justifying experiment) ---
  Objective : show the total error the field reports conflates floor and
              gap, so the decomposition changes the recommended action.
  Method    : reproduce (within tolerance) the per-metric total errors of
              FALCON's net-level GNN and the RF-informed terminal-level
              model on their released data; decompose each into floor
              (partition_floor) + gap (reproduced total - floor).
  Expected  : >=1 metric with a competitive leaderboard error that is
              FLOOR-dominated (so "improve the architecture" is
              misdirected) and >=1 with similar total error that is
              GAP-dominated (a better encoder does help).
  Produces  : Table 4 (SOTA total vs floor vs gap vs verdict).
  Supports  : the paper's operative claim; this is the money experiment.

--- E7  ORACLE VALIDATION OF THE SIMULATION LIMIT ----------------------
  Objective : validate Proposition 1 end to end (a persistent floor =
              variance from factors OUTSIDE the netlist).
  Method    : on a subset, INJECT a known spread of non-netlist variation
              into the labels by re-simulating with controlled device
              mismatch / process corners (or calibrated Monte-Carlo);
              sweep the injected variance sigma^2_inj.
  Expected  : the estimated R4 floor TRACKS sigma^2_inj across the sweep
              (Eq. 5), and NO encoder in the ladder, incl. GraphGPS,
              beats it. This exhibits the mechanism of a persistent floor
              rather than inferring it.
  Supports  : Proposition 1; de-risks the "requires simulation" claim.
  Note      : if no injection is run, report the persistent-floor
              question as OPEN (do not assert a persistent floor).

--- E8  ESTIMATOR ROBUSTNESS (instrument validation) ------------------
  Objective : show the FLOOR is a property of the data, not a knob.
  Method    : (a) recompute the R2 floor across device-value BIN WIDTHS;
              (b) check partition vs neighborhood estimators AGREE within
              CIs where coverage is adequate;
              (c) cross-check with an information-theoretic irreducible-
              error estimator (kNN mutual information, in the spirit of
              Yuan & Lozano-Duran 2025) on the same partition;
              (d) COVERAGE CONVERGENCE: Phi_hat stabilizes as N grows;
              (e) LABEL PRECISION: re-estimate under added label
              quantization to rule out a label-noise artifact.
  Expected  : floor stable across (a)-(e); disagreements reported.
  Supports  : the instrument produces the floor, not a modeling choice.

3. WHAT "PROMISING" vs "NEGATIVE" LOOKS LIKE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMISING (paper as framed):
  - E1 passes.
  - At least one property has a near-zero floor at some rung AND at least
    one property (leading candidate: noise figure on FALCON) retains a
    clearly-nonzero floor at R4, with CIs that separate the two regimes.
    The split is the paper.
PARTIALLY NEGATIVE (still publishable, reframe):
  - Every studied property reaches a near-zero floor by R4 on both
    benchmarks. Then the honest finding is "for these families the graph
    is sufficient for these specs; the open problem is the gap" -- retitle
    around that. Note this is unlikely for noise figure, which is
    physically the strongest high-floor candidate.
NEGATIVE (do not force):
  - Floors are dominated by low coverage at every fine rung and the
    neighborhood cross-check disagrees wildly. Then you cannot claim a
    clean boundary; report the measurement limitation and consider a
    denser-collision subset before submission.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FLAG-BACK TRIGGERS (stop and reconsider)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  * E1 does not come out easy                     -> pipeline bug.
  * gap < 0 by more than estimation noise         -> floor overestimated
                                                     (low coverage) or
                                                     targets mis-scaled.
  * permutation-null floor / total-var far from 1 -> keying leaks P.
  * partition and neighborhood estimates diverge  -> report both; do not
    at a rung with adequate coverage                pick the convenient one.
  * a new 2026-2027 paper covers the floor/gap     -> re-run the Phase-1
    split for circuits                               novelty search; re-angle.
